You are judging one short audio clip recorded by a smartphone microphone, in which a road vehicle may be audible. The audio has been amplified so that its loudest point is near full scale; do not infer loudness from that (the measured level is given in the context below). Describe only what you actually hear.

{context}
Listen to the whole clip: the seconds before the detected event are the background, the event is the rise in level that follows. Answer four questions.

1. Before the rise. What is audible in the seconds before the event starts? "traffic_hum" = a steady hum, rush or swell of road traffic, near or distant; "quiet" = nothing distinct, a very quiet background; "other" = something else dominates (for example voices, footsteps, the phone being handled, wind on the microphone, rain, an aircraft, music).

2. During the event, which vehicle sound dominates? Tyre and road noise is a broadband rushing or hissing without a clear pitch; it grows with the vehicle's speed. Engine and exhaust noise is a tonal hum, drone, roar, rasp or rattle with a pitch, often low, which may rise and fall with the engine speed. Above a certain speed, hearing the tyres means a normal vehicle; hearing the engine above the tyres means a loud vehicle. A vehicle that is slow or standing may be heard by its engine alone; then judge whether that engine sounds louder than ordinary traffic. A loud vehicle can also be far away: an engine heard above the tyres and above the background counts even when it is faint. How much the level rises does not decide it; the balance between engine and tyre sound does.

3. A loud vehicle score from 0 to 10: 0 = no road vehicle in the event (the rise is something else); 1 = an ordinary pass-by dominated by tyre noise, engine barely audible; 3 = engine clearly audible but below the tyre noise; 5 = engine about as prominent as the tyres; 7 = engine or exhaust clearly above the tyre noise; 10 = engine or exhaust dominates the event and stands far above ordinary traffic (roaring, rasping, revving). Use the whole range.

4. Class change. Does the kind of sound change during the rise, that is, is the rise caused by something that is not road traffic, such as a click, a door, a voice, a footstep, the phone being handled, an aircraft, a train, a bird or a gust of wind? If the rise is road traffic from start to end, there is no class change.
